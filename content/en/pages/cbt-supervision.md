I am a **certified CBT supervisor** — *Thérapeute Praticien Superviseur* (TPS), a qualification recognised by the **AFTCC** (French Association for Behavioural and Cognitive Therapy) — and I offer **clinical supervision in CBT** for practising therapists and trainees.

### Who this is for

- **Trainees and therapists in training at the AFTCC** who need documented supervision hours for their programme
- **Qualified psychologists and therapists** looking for a regular, reflective space
- Practitioners working toward **AFTCC certification**
- Therapists wanting to **consolidate their technical CBT skills**, or to integrate **ACT** into their practice
- French-speaking practitioners working abroad who are cut off from a francophone supervision network

Supervision takes place **online**, which makes it workable wherever you practise. Sessions can be conducted in **English or French**.

### What we can work on

- **Case formulation** and cognitive-behavioural conceptualisation
- Selecting and applying **CBT techniques** appropriate to the presentation
- **Complex clinical situations** and therapeutic impasses
- **Ethical dilemmas** and questions of boundaries and frame
- The **therapeutic alliance** and relational difficulties in the room
- Integrating **ACT**, mindfulness, or skills drawn from **DBT**
- Developing your **professional identity** and clinical confidence
- **Preventing burnout** and managing the emotional load of the work

### How I approach supervision

For me, supervision is far more than a regulatory requirement. It is a **protected space** — time deliberately set aside to think about your practice, ask your questions, and notice your own development over time.

I have been in **continuous monthly supervision since qualifying**, with professionals trained in CBT and ACT. I still take part in it because that is where I slow down, think carefully about my work, and get support on complex cases. It has helped me sharpen my clinical reasoning, strengthen my technique, navigate ethical and relational challenges, and stay grounded when the work feels heavy. The supervisors who trained me shaped both how I work with clients and how I now support other therapists.

Feedback is offered with **care, respect, and attention to your stage of development**. You are a developing professional, not a set of techniques to be corrected.

My [longer article on CBT supervision](/blog/cbt-supervision-for-therapists/) sets this out in more detail.
